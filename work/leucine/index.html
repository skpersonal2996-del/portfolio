01

The challenge

Context

Leucine helped pharmaceutical companies digitize their SOPs, from creation and approval to execution on the manufacturing floor.

However, the existing SOP builder was limited to basic checklists and input fields, while real manufacturing procedures required more flexibility.

Key challenges

  • Limited input options

    Operators needed to enter temperatures, dates and measurements, select options and upload evidence, not just tick checkboxes.

  • Missing visual guidance

    SOPs often required images and videos to explain how a task should be performed.

  • Fixed task sequences

    Some steps depended on previous actions, while others needed to be skipped or repeated.

  • Audit and traceability

    Every action, input and deviation needed to be properly recorded and, where required, approved.

02

Research & discovery

To understand how pharmaceutical teams created and executed SOPs, I combined on-site observation, customer conversations and document analysis.

How I gathered insights

  • Plant visits

    Observed operators following physical SOPs, recording inputs, adding remarks and signing off tasks.

  • Stakeholder conversations

    Joined client meetings with PMs and customer success, gathering requirements and pain points from supervisors, floor managers and department heads.

  • SOP analysis

    Collected and compared physical SOP documents from different companies to understand common structures and variations.

Key insight

Despite differences in their procedures, most companies followed a common hierarchy:

  1. Job
  2. Stages
  3. Tasks
  4. Optional subtasks

However, the level of detail and task requirements varied across companies.

This led us to design a common SOP structure with flexibility at the task level, rather than creating a rigid template for every customer.

03

Evolving the SOP experience

I approached the redesign incrementally, starting with the underlying structure and then introducing capabilities based on real customer requirements.

Design decisions

DecisionWhat I designedWhy it mattered
Move beyond basic inputs Created a reusable library of structured inputs: dates, selections, measurements, text and supporting media. Gave supervisors more flexibility to create SOPs and operators clearer ways to record information.
Support non-linear workflows Designed conditional steps and repeat-task workflows. Also introduced scheduled tasks as an additional capability. Made SOPs more adaptable to real manufacturing procedures and recurring operations.
Strengthen execution and accountability Introduced digital signatures, deviation approvals, pause reasons and role-specific inboxes. Improved traceability, audit records and visibility for supervisors.

04

Designing for the manufacturing floor

As customers increasingly shifted towards tablets, the existing interface created usability issues. Operators worked while standing, moving around plants and wearing protective equipment.

What I changed

I adapted the execution experience for tablet use by reorganizing layouts, simplifying navigation, improving table visibility and repositioning important actions.

Result

Following the redesign and release, customer success reported a substantial reduction in recurring tablet-related complaints and support tickets.

05

Building consistency across products

Alongside the redesign, I independently owned Leucine’s design system.

The problem

Two products in the suite had evolved with different UI foundations, components and interaction patterns. As the product suite grew, this created inconsistencies for customers using multiple products.

What I did

I progressively migrated the second product to the more comprehensive existing design system, standardizing components, creating variants, documenting usage guidelines and expanding the library as new features were introduced.

Result

The system became a shared reference for designers and developers, creating a more consistent foundation across products.

06

From design to impact

I owned the design process for both SOP modules, from research and wireframes to usability testing and delivery.

Working with the team

I worked closely with developers and QA, prepared annotated Zeplin handoffs, reviewed implementation builds and addressed edge cases. After release, I worked with product and analytics teams to understand feature usage and customer feedback.

Impact

The changes made a difference in three important ways:

  • More structured execution and audit records

    Operators could follow clearer procedures, capture appropriate inputs and document deviations through more controlled workflows.

  • Better operational visibility

    Supervisors could monitor progress and review pending approvals more effectively.

  • Customer confidence and expansion

    One customer initially tested the redesigned experience at a single plant, then rolled it out across additional plants, including locations where they hadn’t previously subscribed.

The tablet redesign also led to a substantial reduction in recurring tablet-related support complaints, based on feedback from customer success.

07

What I learned

Designing enterprise software means understanding more than the interface. It means understanding the real-world processes, the people responsible for them and the consequences of every action.

At Leucine, I learned how to translate complex, highly controlled workflows into flexible product experiences without losing sight of usability, consistency or traceability.
